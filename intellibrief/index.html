IntelliBrief

A news reader that ends, remembers, and can tell you why each story is in front of you.

Open in your browser iPhone and iPad (TestFlight)

No ads. Without an account you can read a small sample; sign in for free to get your own edition.

Why another news app?

Most news apps are built to keep you scrolling. Stories arrive as isolated headlines, repeat each other across outlets, and are ranked for reasons nobody shows you. IntelliBrief started from the opposite idea: a personal intelligence officer that briefs you and then lets you go.

It ends

One daily edition and a Today page that finishes with “you’re caught up”. No infinite feed. A quiet day is allowed to look quiet.

It remembers

Reports are merged into stories that last for weeks, keeping their facts, sources and timeline, and telling you what changed since your last visit.

It explains itself

Every item can say why it is there. The reason comes from the relevance calculation, never from a language model inventing one afterwards.

Stories with a memory

Follow how a story developed, not just today’s headline

Related reports from many outlets are merged into one story. Open it and you see the current state, how it developed month by month with dated links to the reporting, the earlier stories that led here, and a full timeline.

Follow a story or keep a private idea about it, and come back later to see what changed since you last looked, with dated evidence for and against.

Themes

Today’s news, connected to the reporting you forgot

A theme ties together stories that belong to one larger development, sometimes months apart. It shows the earliest reporting next to the newest, so you can see where today’s event came from.

Each theme gets a short brief, a picture and a timeline you can open story by story.

The constellation

See what stands out today, and how it connects

IntelliBrief keeps track of the people, organisations and places named in every story, more than a thousand on a typical day. The constellation maps the ones that stand out and links those that share stories.

“9 newsrooms, usually 2” tells you a subject is getting unusual attention, which matters more than the raw number. Switch to what is most read, what you follow, or your own topics.

Honest signals

How many newsrooms, not how many articles

A story repeated by fifty sites that copy one agency report is still one report. Every card says how many independent newsrooms reported it, so you can tell a widely covered event from a single claim.

Pictures name what they show, who took them and under which licence. AI illustrations are labelled as such.

Where things stand

Official figures beside the news

When the news is about interest rates, IntelliBrief shows what the central banks actually published: the policy rate, when it last changed, and the weekly balance-sheet figures compared with a week and a year ago.

Every figure names its source and date, taken from the European Central Bank, the Federal Reserve and the Bank of England.

Principles

Sources first

AI helps select and summarise; it does not replace the evidence. Every summary links back to the original reporting.

Licensed content only

Every source is checked for permitted use before it is collected. Publishers that do not allow it are left out.

Measured before shown

Anything a model extracts must pass a measured accuracy check before a reader sees it. Where the app cannot say why, it says nothing.

Funded by readers

No ads and no sponsored placement. Your follows, places and reading decide what comes first.
